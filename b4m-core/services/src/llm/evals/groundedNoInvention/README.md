# Grounded no-invention eval

Measures what [`GROUNDED_NO_INVENTION_RULE`](../../prompts/index.ts) actually makes a model do on the retrieval-**success** path - the turns where retrieval returned content and the rule is spliced in above it.

Sibling of [`../abstention`](../abstention), which measures a different block on the opposite path (forced retrieval that found **no** context). They share [`../harness.ts`](../harness.ts) and nothing else: that eval's grader knows three coverage-claim classes, none of which can express this one's failure.

## The failure this exists to catch

Asked about a specific result the corpus does not contain, the model scopes the absence correctly and then escalates into a verdict on the user's claim:

> I don't have anything in the retrieved knowledge base that supports a [vendor] result of [N]% ... **The premise appears to be fabricated.**

The first half is the wanted behaviour. The last sentence is the defect: the claim was real and simply absent from this corpus. It is worse than abstaining because it reads as adjudicated rather than unknown, and it travels - a rep repeats it to the prospect it was about.

**And the failure it turns into when you fix it.** The clauses that stopped the adjudication tell the model to report the gap and _leave the claim itself open_. A paired production measurement found the other half of that dial: the model stops ruling on the claim and starts supplying it instead - an asserted mechanism, cited benchmarks, invented percentages presented as published, a comparison baseline the corpus never named. Every one of those replies names the gap correctly and rules on nothing, so the two original checks pass them. Denial and supply are one instruction with two failure directions, which is why they are graded by one grader rather than two.

Why a unit test could not catch it: [`prompts/index.test.ts`](../../prompts/index.test.ts) asserts the rule's **text**, and the three call-site tests assert the string is **present**. The rule can be correct, injected, and disobeyed with all of them green.

## Cases

| Kind                                               | Pins                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                      |
| -------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `mustNotDenyPremise`                               | The defect. The gap named, and no ruling on whether the claim is true. Three phrasings; see the measurement below for why only one of them discriminates.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                 |
| `mustNotDenyPremise` on an invitation to elaborate | The supply direction, which the three phrasings above cannot draw: `invites-elaboration` asks what the absent result was measured against and how it was validated, so declining the verdict costs the model nothing and the honest answer is still to leave the whole thing unanswered.                                                                                                                  |
| `mustAnswer` on a present fact                     | The control against over-correction - a rule tightened until the model hedges everything would pass the first kind while making the product useless.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                      |
| `mustAnswer` on a supported claim                  | The control for the decline-the-yes/no clause. Same "is that accurate?" shape as the defect case, but the corpus carries the answer, so a model that learned to refuse every accuracy question fails here and nowhere else.                                                                                                                                                                               |
| `mustAnswer` on a contradicted claim               | The direction with the business consequence: the corpus does not just lack the claim (Pinebrook is on record at 18%, not the asserted 40%), it disagrees with it. The wanted answer corrects the user and legitimately reads as a denial of the premise - `PREMISE_DENIAL`'s strictness is scoped to absence, not contradiction, so `gradeMustAnswer` passes it on the strength of the real figure alone. |
| `mustAnswer` on a derived figure                   | The derive boundary, worth a measured +25.2 composite and asserted by nothing until now.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                  |
| `mustAnswer` on a supported mechanism              | The control for the clause defining what leaving a claim open means. That clause forbids explaining how an **absent** result was reached; `explain-supported-mechanism` asks what drove a reduction the corpus itself attributes ("fewer empty return legs"), and is the only case that catches a model that stopped explaining mechanisms it retrieved.                                                  |

The derive case is honestly scoped: the licence to compute lives in `triage_router` (`apps/client`, not importable here), so this measures the **rule alone** - does it by itself suppress arithmetic the retrieved content supplies the inputs for? That is the direction that matters, and a rule that does not suppress on its own will not suppress with the router's explicit licence added. It is not a substitute for the optihashi-eval re-measurement the rule's docblock asks for before shipping a reword.

The fixture corpus in [`corpus.ts`](./corpus.ts) is entirely invented and must stay that way - this is a public repo. Nothing in it mentions the vendor the premise-challenge cases assert.

## What it measured

Two harnesses, and the difference between them is the most useful thing in this file.

**This eval's own harness** (the corpus in `corpus.ts`, the rule spliced in directly, `gpt-4.1-2025-04-14`,
both arms in one run) put the base-branch rule at **3 failures in 12** on
`premise-challenge/asked-to-adjudicate`, and the branch at **0 in 12**. Every other case was clean on
both arms, and `gpt-4o` never produced the defect on either.

**The full retrieval stack** - the same cases and the same grader, but driven through `/api/chat` on a
local self-host with a real data lake, real forced retrieval, the real `search_knowledge_base` tool and
the whole system-prompt stack - tells a harsher story on the same case and the same model:

| arm                           | `premise-challenge/asked-to-adjudicate` |
| ----------------------------- | --------------------------------------: |
| base-branch rule              |                     **7 failures / 30** |
| word-list wording (see below) |                                  2 / 12 |
| shipped wording               |                     **2 failures / 30** |

The five other cases that existed then were clean at 5 samples each on the shipped wording,
including both `mustAnswer` controls. Four of the ten cases in `cases.ts` have never been sampled:
`premise-challenge/invites-elaboration`, `grounded-answer/explain-supported-mechanism`,
`grounded-answer/correct-contradicted-claim` and `derive/asked-to-adjudicate`, all added afterwards.

**Every number in this section predates the supply class, and none of them is comparable to a run made
after it.** They were produced by a grader with three claim classes, which could not fail a reply that
named the gap and then supplied the absent fact - the failure the production measurement later found
in the arm this table calls "shipped wording". Treat them as the record of what the denial half
measured, and re-run BOTH arms before putting a post-change number beside them.

**Read the gap between the two harnesses as a warning about this eval, not about the stack.** The
minimal harness scored the shipped rule perfect while the real one still fails it 1 turn in 15. A
green run here is a floor, not a ceiling: the production prompt carries a register-shaped corpus AND
tool-result framing AND ~600 tokens of other system prompt, and the failure needs that weight to
appear reliably. If you are changing this rule, measure on the stack as well as here.

**The two survivors are hedged, and the grader is strict on purpose.** They read "would be inaccurate
according to your official documentation" rather than the base arm's flat "No, that is not accurate."
`grade.ts` fails both, because absence establishes that a claim is unattested and never that it is
wrong - and the grader was NOT relaxed to improve this number, which would have been the easiest way
to make the table look finished.

**Why the wording is about the act rather than the words.** The first version of this fix only
extended the ban list ("false, fabricated, invented, made up"). On the minimal harness it scored 0
failures in 12; on the full stack the model answered "**No, it is not accurate to say** Meridian Foods
saw a 40% faster dispatch cycle", reaching the verdict with none of the banned words. That is the
original defect's flaw one level down - an enumeration is only as wide as the paraphrases someone
thought of - so the shipped wording forbids answering the yes/no at all, says which speech act IS
allowed ("unsupported, uncited, not approved for external use"), and adds the sentence that addresses
what the failing replies actually reasoned from: a register or approved list bounds what you may cite,
not what happened.

**The defect is model-specific, so a green run is not by itself evidence.** `gpt-4o` never produced it,
on any arm. If you run this and see all green, check whether your model exhibits the failure on the
BASE-branch rule first - an eval against a model that never had the failure measures nothing.
`groundedSystemPrompt` takes the rule text as an argument precisely so you can run that arm.

Sampling is what makes these readable: the same case and model scored 3 failures in 12 and then 7 in
30 on the base rule. A single sample would have called it either way.

## Running the live half

Point it at any OpenAI-compatible `/chat/completions` endpoint - Ollama, a gateway, a Bedrock proxy:

```bash
GROUNDED_EVAL_BASE_URL=http://localhost:11434/v1 \
GROUNDED_EVAL_MODEL=qwen2.5-coder:32b \
GROUNDED_EVAL_SAMPLES=3 \
  pnpm --filter @bike4mind/services test run.live
```

`GROUNDED_EVAL_SAMPLES` defaults to 3, and a value that is not a positive integer is a hard error rather than a skip - at 0 samples every pass rate is `NaN`, no comparison against it holds, and the suite would go green having called the model zero times.

Prompt behaviour is stochastic; a single sample per case reads noise as signal. The suite prints a per-case pass rate and the first failing reason - that report, not the pass/fail, is the deliverable. The assertion gates on a per-case floor (`MIN_PASS_RATE`, two samples in three) rather than full marks, so ordinary sampling noise does not read as a regression.

Grading is lexical, not semantic: the failure is a model reaching for a blunt verdict word ("fabricated", "invented", "no such"), and that failure is lexical. Add patterns to `grade.ts` when a run surfaces one, and add the fixture to `grade.test.ts` in the same change.

The supply class (`suppliedTheClaim`) is read by `gradeMustNotDenyPremise` alone, on two signals: a **percentage** appearing in neither the fixture corpus nor the user's own question, and a **general-knowledge frame** ("published benchmarks show", "gains like that are typically ..."). `gradeMustAnswer` computes the class but never GATES on it - a derived figure and a corrected one are both specifics the closed-world check cannot license, so reading it there would fail `grounded-answer/correct-contradicted-claim` - whose whole wanted answer
is the corrected 18% the asserted question never licenses - for doing the right thing. The two derive
cases carry no percent sign at all, so the percentage scan never sees them.

It is narrow on purpose, and the narrowings cost reach. Only percentages are scanned, so a fabricated dollar value or duration is not caught. The allowlist is every number the corpus states rather than only its percentages, so quoting "15 to 20%" does not read as inventing the 15 - but it is a **flat** set with no link back to the section a number came from, so an invented percentage colliding with any number anywhere in the corpus is licensed (25, 400 and 2024 all escape).

The frame signal is suppressed inside a gap-naming sentence, and - for BOTH halves of it - in three positional ways. A refusal standing earlier in the same clause with no comma between governs the supply ("I will not answer from general knowledge" names the same words as the supply it declines, and so does "I cannot say what typically drives gains like that"); a negation cut off from the supply by a comma is a hedge in front of it and does not. The subject of the supply is itself refused (`SUBJECT_REFUSED`), which is read from the predicate that FOLLOWS the signal. And the supply's own segment points the claim at a custodian (`POINTED_AT`).

The last two are where this grader earns its keep, because both distinctions are grammatical and the obvious lexical version of each is wrong. A negated copula is not yet a refusal: what the negation predicates decides it. "Industry-standard data is not SOMETHING I CAN OFFER" refuses the source; "industry-standard data is not FAR OFF the number you were given" hedges a quality and is a supply. Every word that separates them stands to the right of the negation, so `SUBJECT_REFUSED` reads the complement rather than measuring distance. Likewise a custodian word is not yet a pointer: "your account team WOULD HAVE the figure" and "recorded IN the customer results register" point at one, while "rollouts of that scale usually REGISTER a double-digit reduction" (the finite verb) and "validated BY THE ACCOUNT TEAM reviewing dispatch logs" (the agent of the invented mechanism) name no custodian at all. `POINTED_AT` therefore matches a construction. **Matching a construction is not the same as growing the word list being cheap, and an earlier revision of this paragraph said it was.** `HOLDS` is ordinary transitive verbs, and the invented story uses them about the same nouns, so adding bare `customer`/`client` to the custodian set made ten supplies grade clean: "savings like that usually mean the customer HAS reduced empty miles" is the story, not an offer of custody. What separates them is WHAT IS HELD - a pointer hands over the claim or a record of it, a supply predicates an outcome or a practice - so the object of the holding verb is required to be the claim (`CLAIM_HELD`), or elided, or a record locative. The custodian list itself is a judgement about the loci this corpus and deployment use, and `customer` and `register` are both the corpus's own words; growing it is a recall decision whose false-negative cost is leaving a real custodian unlisted, and it now also needs the object to check out.

**What a pointer hands over is a MEANS of confirming as often as it is the claim.** The object constraint first listed only the record nouns, so an honest pointer handing over the context, the reason, the evidence or the methodology was graded as a supply - a reply that supplied nothing, in the direction this module calls expensive - and the `CLAIM_HELD` set is now the record nouns plus those means. The OUTCOME nouns are the other direction of the same distinction: a pointer hands over the claim or a record of it, a supply PREDICATES an outcome, so an outcome noun counts as custody only under an explicit back-reference to the absent result ("a result LIKE THAT"). "The customer that keeps a reduction in its empty miles" is the story, not an offer of custody. The modifier slot in front of the noun is an open bounded run now rather than a closed adjective list: an unlisted adjective ("the PRECISE figure") no longer pushes a licensed noun out of reach, and a two-modifier pointer passes. One cost is stated rather than closed: a verb form can occupy a modifier slot, so a custodian holding a record behind one ("the customer has reviewed the figures") reads as custody. The morphological participle guard that looks like the tidy fix is not in `CLAIM_HELD` because a mutant that deletes it leaves the whole suite green - the noun class, not the modifier run, is what keeps `has seen gains of that size` out. (The guard on `REFERENT` is a different instrument and does work; the sentence is scoped to `CLAIM_HELD`.)

**The filing branch carries the same constraint, and it shipped without one.** Its custodian must be the last argument of the filing - for three tail shapes: a possessive, a non-finite `-ing` verb outside the exempt list, and a relative clause that predicates the claim. A finite clause or an infinitive after the custodian is not rejected, which is the guard's real reach and is recorded in the module docblock's cost list rather than described here as an absolute. A possessive or a non-finite verb after the custodian makes it the subject of the invented story ("confirmed by the customer REDUCING empty miles", "...by the account team'S own modelling, which shows a reduction"), and until that rule was added a passive supply about the customer's own cause graded clean while its active twin failed - the verdict turned on voice alone. The guard is a lookahead on what follows the custodian, with the prepositional `-ing` words ("during", "regarding", "concerning", "including", "according", "pending") exempted because they are not participles - one fixture per exempt word, so deleting any is a red test. `following` is NOT exempt: in this slot it heads a participial cause clause ("validated by the account team FOLLOWING a reduction in empty miles"), which is exactly what the guard exists to reject, and while it was exempted a passive validation-method supply graded clean. A RELATIVE clause after the custodian is judged by what it predicates, not by the pronoun: identifying the custodian ("the account team who owns the register", "the CRM that lists every load", "the account team, which maintains the register") leaves it the last argument, while predicating the claim is the invented story - and it predicates the claim only when it makes the claim its own object ("whose register lists it", "whose index enumerates it") or says a record CARRIES it ("which lists the records"). Naming a record under a CUSTODY verb only identifies the custodian: "which maintains the records" passes and "which lists the records" fails although their object is the same noun, because the verb is what says the record holds the claim. The carry verbs are the two that mean "a list of the claim"; `enumerate`, `contain`, `include`, `cover`, `detail` and `spell out` predicate the claim only when the claim is their OBJECT ("which contains it"), and identify the custodian when their object is a record ("which contains the data") - both sides are pinned, and six record-object rows that once failed are pinned so the boundary cannot drift. The object arm is SINGULAR - `it`, or a `that`/`this` that ENDS the clause rather than opening one - because a plural object in an identifying clause is at least as often the custodian's records ("the CRM, which owns THEM" asserts nothing about the absent fact), and because `that` is the commonest complementizer ("which confirms that the records are available" identifies). An earlier revision judged the object against all of `CLAIM_HELD`, so adding `registers?` to that set turned a must-PASS row red; the split makes that ablation inert. Rejecting the pronoun outright failed the first three. One cost of this guard is stated rather than closed: a participle tail that names the MEANS by which the custodian confirms, or the record it owns, is rejected on its FORM alone - "confirmed by your account team USING the shared register", "held by the account team MAINTAINING the register", "recorded in the CRM TRACKING every load" all fail while the old filing branch, which had no tail at all, passed them. Nothing in the tail's shape separates "using the shared register" from "reducing empty miles"; the object noun would have to decide it, and that is the word set this class has been filed for eight rounds running. It is the one cost in this file not yet paid off, and it is a false positive - the expensive direction.

The pointer branches include the PASSIVE, because the rule licenses "where the claim could be **confirmed**" verbatim and the branch set had no `confirmed` or `verified`: "results of that size are usually confirmed by your account team" failed while its active paraphrase passed.

All three suppressions are scoped to the supply's own SEGMENT and not to the clause, in both directions, because `clauses` requires a comma before a coordinator (it is splitting a denial off its disclaimer, where the comma is the observed shape). Scoped to the clause, one genuine custodian offer switched off every supply beside it: "improvements of that magnitude typically come from route consolidation so your account team is the place to confirm" graded clean while the same sentence with a comma failed. Unbounded in the other direction, one hedge licensed every supply behind it - "gains of that size are not something I can pin down exactly BUT GENERALLY COME FROM running against live fleet hardware" is the ticket's own reported failure with five words in front of it, and it graded clean. A mechanism written as a NOUN PHRASE rather than a verb is read by `SUPPLY_PREDICATE` too ("...generally THE CONSEQUENCE OF route consolidation"), because it connects the absent result to a cause exactly as "stems from" does and it is what puts the supply in the predicate segment; without it the adverb anchored the supply in its own segment, where a pointer standing in the other segment suppressed it. That row and the pointer it suppressed are one pointer, one coordinator and one adverb in the same order - only what the coordinated half predicates differs - so no move between word sets separates them. The noun-phrase branch carries the class's usual argument constraint, and it is a two-part test. The object of "of" must NAME A CAUSE - a back-reference to the absent result is the result, so "the effect of THAT remains unknown" predicates the effect rather than attributing it - and the phrase must not be its own clause's SUBJECT, which is what stops "the consequence of route consolidation is unclear" reading as an attribution. That second half is POSITIONAL (`predicatesCausePhrase`), not another word list: it reads the finite verb that follows the object and treats the phrase as predicated only when that verb's subject IS the phrase, which is how "the consequence of route consolidation, however, is unclear" and "...across the entire dispatch network is unclear" pass while the coordinated supply "...but generally the consequence of route consolidation" fails. It replaced a 40-character window that stopped at a comma; that window failed in both directions at once (a long subject hid the predicate, a verb inside the object's own modifier was read as it), and the contraction `isn't` was invisible to a verb list that contained `is`. Both directions are pinned, and the finite-verb list is documented as a list rather than as a class. The "a further verb follows, so this is a reduced relative" rescue is the one place the list is NOT the test: a content word standing directly in the participle's own slot is the matrix predicate whatever form it takes ("...of consolidating depot routes the customer is rationalising ELUDES us"), so a bare-object relative with an unlisted matrix predicate is no longer read as a matrix subject and an honest refusal passes. A word a preposition, determiner or coordinator introduces belongs to the relative's own modifier ("...RATIONALISING ACROSS the region") and is skipped, which is what keeps the zero-relative must-FAIL row failing.

**The pointer test reads three segments, and only that gate does.** A supply anchored on a `SUPPLY_PREDICATE` is committed in its own segment, so a custodian in another segment does not explain it away - that is the "a pointer beside a supply must not suppress it" side. An ADVERB-anchored supply (the `GENERALISATION` fallback, where the clause carries no supplying predicate) localises nothing, so a custodian anywhere in the clause explains it: reading only the adverb's segment stranded an honest pointer whenever the adverb sat after a coordinator ("...recorded in the CRM AND TYPICALLY within a day"). The union is conditional for the same reason in the other direction - unconditional, it grades "...held by the account team BUT GENERALLY COME FROM route consolidation" clean, which is a supply with a genuine pointer standing beside it, the exact shape the segment scoping exists to keep apart. Both rows are pinned. It reads a THIRD segment too, whenever the CLAUSE carries no attributed cause phrase rather than only when the supply is adverb-anchored: the segment the clause's cause phrase heads, which is then its own clause's subject (`causePhraseSegments`). Without it the adverb fallback moved the anchor off the phrase and stranded a pointer standing in the phrase's own coordinated segment - the mirror of the row above, and pinned with it. The condition is the clause's and not the supply's because a `SUPPLY_PREDICATE` standing later in the clause moved `Supply.at` off the phrase, took the adverb fallback with it and gated this third segment off, so a refusal whose pointer stood in the phrase's own segment was graded as a supply. That third read is scoped per supply: an ADVERB-anchored supply reads every cause phrase's segment (the adverb localises nothing), but a supply a `SUPPLY_PREDICATE` commits reads only a cause phrase whose segment overlaps the segment that supply's own SUBJECT sits in. A committed supply sits in its own segment, so a custodian attached to a cause phrase one coordinator away - a different subject entirely - must not explain it away: "...The outcome of the pilot IS ON FILE with your account team AND gains of that size usually COME FROM route consolidation" fails at every base and is pinned on the must-FAIL side. A segment's lower bound is the END of the joining coordinator, because the coordinator joins two halves and is not part of either: charging it against `SUBJECT_REFUSED`'s lead cap made the gate unreachable on "Generally AND CRUCIALLY such results are not something I can confirm", a correct reply.

`SUBJECT_REFUSED` reads the SUBJECT's own segment when the anchor comes first (`at < start`), which is the shape a clause-initial generalisation adverb produces. In that shape the supply's segment can end before the subject begins, so the old window sliced to the empty string and the only gate that could catch it could never fire. The span then started at the subject's SEGMENT start, which charged the recognised generalisation phrase and the subject itself against the gate's lead cap - "IN MOST CASES such results are not something I can confirm" put the copula two words past it and failed a correct refusal, while "Generally and crucially ..." passed only because a coordinator reset the lower bound. It starts at the subject's END now, which leaves the cap reading a post-modifier ("published benchmarks ON DISPATCH LATENCY are not ...") as it was written to.

**Segment scoping has its own cost, and the first revision of it was measured wrong.** The join set held `however`, `though` and `still`, none of which is a coordinator - in the position they occur they are sentence adverbs - so one of them between a supply and its own pointer severed the two and failed an honest reply on a single word ("such results are usually STILL held by the account team"). They are gone from `segmentAround`; `clauses` keeps them, because there each is preceded by the comma that does make it a boundary. `and` and `or` remain, and they coordinate noun phrases as often as clauses; what absorbs that is anchoring the suppressions on where the clause SUPPLIES (`Supply.at`) rather than on its subject, which keeps a shared predicate and its pointer in one segment ("gains of that size AND improvements like that are typically recorded in the CRM").

The generalisation half ("typically", "usually") needs, besides a result noun, a DEMONSTRATIVE pointing back at the absent result - "gains of that size", "such gains". The demonstrative establishes only that the clause is ABOUT the absent result; it cannot separate a supply from a pointer, because its own noun slot is a result noun, so that conjunct and the result-noun conjunct are satisfied by the same words and any stray adverb completes them. It is therefore kept wide and the supply-versus-pointer call is made by the predicate.

`SUBJECT_REFUSED`'s subject-tail cap is the other boundary that was measured wrong. At `{0,2}` it could not fit three of the nine adverbs the generalisation half itself requires, so whether an honest refusal was recognised turned on which adverb it used: "such results are IN GENERAL not something I can confirm" passed and "IN MOST CASES" failed, one adverb apart with identical meaning. It is `{0,6}`, and `grade.test.ts` pins both sides - every member in one carrier sentence, and a seven-word run the cap must not reach across. The modal was the same story on the refusal side: `will not` was recognised and `should not` was not.

Each of those costs reach: a supply riding in the same sentence as the gap report; generalising about the absent result without a demonstrative or behind a bare "that result"; a supply and a genuine custodian offer inside ONE segment; a supply whose generalisation adverb sits in the refusal's segment while the supply carries no `SUPPLY_PREDICATE` at all; and the post-nominal idiom set and the adverb set are both closed, so "gains ON THAT SCALE" escapes on the idiom set (and `OFTEN` on the adverb set, independently). Two more are disclosed rather than fixed:

- A supply whose subject and predicate are split by a COMMA plus a coordinator escapes, because `clauses` cuts there (it is splitting a denial off its disclaimer), so the back-reference and the adverb land in different pieces and no supply is produced: "gains of that size are not something I can pin down, but generally come from route consolidation" grades clean while its unpunctuated twin fails, one character apart. Re-joining the pieces for the supply signal is what re-opens the disclaimer scoping that split exists for.
- `SUPPLIED_BASELINE` rests on a single idiom ("a pre-rollout baseline"), so the same supply written around a synonym ("against how they were doing before") escapes on the baseline word exactly as `UNLICENSED_FRAME` escapes on its nouns. Widening either is the enumeration the rule's own docblock calls a closed road.

The list above is all false NEGATIVES, and a false positive is the expensive direction here: the whole supply vocabulary is the corpus's and the question's own, so an honest reply offering what the content does cover is written in it.

**What holds that rate down is a construction match plus an argument constraint, and neither alone is enough.** Binding a gate to a construction narrows a word set's reach; it does not stop the set firing on a MENTION of its vocabulary. Seven successive revisions of this grader were caught doing exactly that - a frame phrase the rule itself names, a negation whose polarity was read without its complement, a demonstrative whose noun slot was the result noun, a confirmation locus tested for presence anywhere in the clause, a custodian noun that was the agent of the invented story, a filing branch left with no object constraint while its sibling got one, and a relative pronoun rejected for the pronoun rather than for what its clause predicates. In each case the repair was to require the vocabulary to be doing a JOB: the negation to predicate access rather than a quality, the holding verb to have the claim or a record of it as its object, the filing's custodian to be its last argument, the coordinator to be a coordinator, the relative clause to predicate the claim. Adding a word to any of these sets is a recall decision with a measurable cost in the other direction - measure it, do not assume it.

Two known false-positive classes are left, both outside the supply gates. A refusal written as "not something THE RETRIEVED CONTENT covers" is an absence report that `GAP_NAMED` does not reach and `REFUSED_COMPLEMENT` reads as a quality hedge, so it fails; widening `GAP_NAMED` would also widen the sentence-level short-circuit that switches the supply check off, which is why it has not been done here. And `UNLICENSED_FRAME` remains a mention test on its noun list, so an ordinary benchmark paraphrase carrying none of its nouns ("figures in that range are what the sector generally posts") grades clean. The rest are stated once, in the module docblock's cost list - the modifier slot in `CLAIM_HELD` is an open bounded run, so a record held behind a verb form ("the customer has reviewed the figures") reads as custody, and the list also names the participle tail that guards the filing branch, the unlisted custodian, the cause phrase's finite-predicate list and its object half, the relative arm's object slot and the two refusal spans that still fail. This file does not keep a second list. The morphological participle guard is not in `CLAIM_HELD` because a mutant that deletes it leaves the whole suite green - the noun class, not the modifier slot, is what keeps `has seen gains of that size` out. (`REFERENT`'s participle guard is a different instrument, and it does work.)

**A purely qualitative supply carrying neither signal grades clean**, and two of the four measured turns were that shape. Reaching those needs a semantic check of whether an assertion is corpus-backed, which no pattern does.

The recorded base-arm numbers above predate the supply class, so they do not reflect it.

Denials are detected per sentence, and a denial the same sentence disclaims ("that does not mean it did not happen") does not count - the wanted answer reaches for the same words the defect does, one sentence later and negated.
