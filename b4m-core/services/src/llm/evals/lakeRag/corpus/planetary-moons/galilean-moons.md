# The Galilean Moons of Jupiter

Jupiter's four largest moons - Io, Europa, Ganymede and Callisto - are called the Galilean moons. Galileo Galilei discovered them in January 1610 with an early telescope, and they were the first objects found orbiting a body other than Earth.

Ganymede is the largest moon in the solar system. It is bigger than the planet Mercury and is the only moon known to generate its own magnetic field.

Io is the most volcanically active body in the solar system. Tidal flexing from Jupiter and the neighbouring moons heats its interior and drives hundreds of active volcanoes.

Europa is covered by a shell of water ice. Scientists think a salty liquid ocean lies beneath that shell, which makes Europa a leading target in the search for habitable environments.

Callisto is the most heavily cratered of the four. Its surface is among the oldest in the solar system.

NASA's Juno spacecraft entered orbit around Jupiter on July 4, 2016, and has returned close-up observations of several of the Galilean moons during its extended mission.
