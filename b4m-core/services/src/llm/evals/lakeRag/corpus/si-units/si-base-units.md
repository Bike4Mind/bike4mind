# The SI Base Units

The International System of Units (SI) has seven base units: the second, the metre, the kilogram, the ampere, the kelvin, the mole and the candela. Every other SI unit is derived from these.

Each base unit is now defined by fixing the numerical value of a constant of nature:

- The second is defined by the caesium-133 hyperfine transition frequency, fixed at 9,192,631,770 hertz.
- The metre is defined by the speed of light in vacuum, fixed at 299,792,458 metres per second.
- The mole is defined by the Avogadro constant.
- The kelvin is defined by the Boltzmann constant.

The SI is maintained by the International Bureau of Weights and Measures (BIPM) in Sevres, France, under the Treaty of the Metre, signed in Paris in 1875. The National Institute of Standards and Technology (NIST) is the national metrology institute of the United States.
