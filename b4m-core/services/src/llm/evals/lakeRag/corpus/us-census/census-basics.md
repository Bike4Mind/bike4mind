# The Decennial Census

Article I, Section 2 of the U.S. Constitution requires a count of the population every ten years. The results are used to apportion seats in the House of Representatives among the states.

The first census was taken in 1790, under the direction of Thomas Jefferson as Secretary of State. U.S. marshals carried out the count on horseback.

Census Day, the reference date for the count, is April 1 of the census year. The Census Bureau is part of the Department of Commerce.

The House has had 435 seats since 1913. The Reapportionment Act of 1929 made that size permanent and set the method for reapportioning seats automatically after each census.
